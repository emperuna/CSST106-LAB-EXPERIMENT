## Concepts

- A histogram counts the pixels at each intensity. A low-contrast image crowds them into a narrow band.
- **Global equalization** remaps intensities with the cumulative distribution, spreading them across the full range.
- **Contrast stretching** linearly scales a chosen range of values onto 0-255.
- **CLAHE** equalizes small tiles separately, and limits how far each histogram can be amplified. This curbs the noise boost of global equalization.
- Color images are equalized on brightness only (Y in YUV, or V in HSV), so colors do not shift.

## Procedure

1. Load an image and convert it to grayscale.
2. Compute its histogram with `cv2.calcHist`.
3. Apply `cv2.equalizeHist`, and equalize a color image on its brightness channel.
4. Stretch contrast by scaling pixel values.
5. Apply CLAHE with `cv2.createCLAHE`.
6. Compare each version and its histogram.

For Part 3, this lab includes its own equalizer, written step by step from the histogram and cumulative distribution. Its output is compared with OpenCV's.
